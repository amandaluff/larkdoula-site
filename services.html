Frequently Asked Questions

What does a doula do?

A doula is a trained support person for you and your family through pregnancy, birth, and the early weeks of parenthood. I bring knowledge of what to expect, support you in advocating for yourself and your family, and help you feel confident and cared for along the way. I do not provide medical care, give medical advice, or make decisions for you.

What does support look like before, during, and after the birth?

Before the birth

We usually meet twice, for about 90 minutes each, around 28 to 32 weeks and again at 34 to 36 weeks. At the first visit, we talk through what to expect in labor and birth, comfort and pain relief, interventions, and making decisions, and we start your birth preferences. At the second, we finish your birth preferences and plan for your recovery, feeding, and the first weeks at home. Your support person is part of both visits.

During labor and birth

When your package includes birth support, I'm on call from 37 weeks. When labor starts, or your induction begins, we stay in touch by phone and decide together when I should join you. Many people want me there once labor is active, but you can ask me to come sooner. For a planned cesarean, I join you before the surgery, and in the operating room when the hospital allows. Once I'm with you, I stay through the birth. However your birth happens, I help you and your support person with comfort and decisions. I stay for 1 to 2 hours after the birth to help you settle in and get started with feeding. If I can't be there, a backup doula will support you.

After the birth

Postpartum visits can be at home or virtual. If your package doesn't include birth support, your first postpartum visit can be at the hospital. We talk through your birth, how you're recovering and feeding, and what would help now.

Is a doula still helpful if I'm having an induction, an epidural, or a planned cesarean?

Yes.

With an induction

Inductions can take a long time, especially at the start. We plan ahead for what yours may involve and how to rest and stay comfortable early on. We stay in touch throughout, and I join you once labor is active, or sooner if you'd like.

With an epidural

I can help you think through when you might want one. Once it's in place, I help with position changes, comfort, and rest. I also help you understand what's happening and support you through pushing.

With a cesarean

For a planned cesarean, we prepare your preferences for the birth, such as skin-to-skin and feeding, and I'm with you before surgery and in the operating room when the hospital allows. If a cesarean becomes the plan during labor, I stay with you and your support person as plans change and help you prepare. Afterward, I help with skin-to-skin, feeding, and settling in.

Do you offer payment plans?

Yes. A 50% deposit is due when you sign your agreement. For birth packages, the balance is due by 37 weeks. You can also pay through Afterpay, which splits the cost into smaller payments.

Where do you provide services?

I am happy to meet you for visits in your home, at your hospital, or in a public place. I also offer all prenatal and postpartum visits virtually. I am based in Bay View and serve families across Milwaukee County, including Wauwatosa, Whitefish Bay, Glendale, Greenfield, West Allis, Franklin, Oak Creek, South Milwaukee, and Cudahy. I can attend births at any local hospital or birth center, including Aurora Sinai Medical Center, Aurora West Allis Medical Center, Ascension Columbia St. Mary's Hospital Milwaukee, Ascension St. Joseph, and the Froedtert Hospital Birth Center.
