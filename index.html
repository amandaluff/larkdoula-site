Milwaukee doula for birth, high-risk pregnancy & NICU families

I'm Amanda, and I'm glad you're here!

I'm a certified doula with a doctorate in public health and over a decade of experience in maternal and child health. I believe birth looks different for everyone, and there's no single "right" way to do it. Birth stays with us for the rest of our lives, and I want yours to be a positive start for you and your baby.

I want you to know that I can support you no matter what your pregnancy looks like, whether you're hoping for an unmedicated birth, planning an epidural, or preparing for an induction or a scheduled cesarean (or aren't sure yet what you want). I have particular expertise in supporting families through high-risk pregnancies and NICU admissions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a doula do?

A doula is a trained support person for you and your family through pregnancy, birth, and the early weeks of parenthood. I bring knowledge of what to expect, support you in advocating for yourself and your family, and help you feel confident and comforted. I do not provide medical care, give medical advice, or make decisions for you.

What does support look like before, during, and after the birth?

Prenatal Visits

Before our first visit, you fill out a short worksheet about what matters most to you. I use your answers to plan our visits, so our time goes to your priorities. Prenatal visits are usually about 90 minutes. Core topics I cover include:

  • Comfort and pain relief during labor
  • Making decisions about common interventions
  • Feeding your baby
  • Newborn care
  • Navigating a high-risk pregnancy

Labor and Birth

When your package includes birth support, I'm on call from 37 weeks. When labor starts, or your induction begins, we stay in touch by phone and decide together when I should join you. Many people want me there once labor is active, but you can ask me to come sooner. For a planned cesarean, I join you before the surgery, and in the operating room when the hospital allows. Once I'm with you, I stay through the birth. During the birth, I help you stay comfortable and support you and your support person as you make decisions. I stay for 1 to 2 hours after the birth to help you settle in and get started with feeding. If I can't be there, a backup doula will support you.

Postpartum Visits

Postpartum visits can be at home, in the hospital, or virtual, and last up to 2 hours. Depending on what you need, we can talk through your birth and how you are feeling, troubleshoot feeding (breast, pump, or bottle), or go over questions about caring for your baby. I can also help in practical ways, like watching your baby so you can rest or shower.

Is a doula still helpful if I'm having an induction, an epidural, or a planned cesarean?

Yes! Support matters just as much when your birth includes an induction, an epidural, or a cesarean.

With an induction, support often starts before labor, with talking through what the process might look like (inductions can take a long time) and staying in touch by phone until you want me there in person. With an epidural, the focus is on comfort and understanding what's happening. For a planned cesarean, support can include writing your preferences for the birth (like skin-to-skin and feeding) and being with you in the operating room when the hospital allows.

How do you support high-risk or complicated pregnancies?

A high-risk pregnancy often comes with more appointments, more tests, and more decisions. We'll start with what you already know about your diagnosis and the questions you have. Support can include:

  • Learning how your diagnosis affects your body
  • Talking through your care team's plan and how you feel about it
  • Finding answers to your questions
  • Virtual or in-person support for additional tests and procedures
  • Using informed decision-making steps to work through your choices
