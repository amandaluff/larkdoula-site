NICU Doula & Parent Support

Babies go to the NICU for many reasons: being born early, trouble breathing, low blood sugar, an infection, or a diagnosis like a heart condition. About 1 in 10 babies spend time there, some for a few days and others for months. Whatever brought you here, this support is for you.

The NICU team cares for your baby. I'm here for you.

I give you space to talk through how you're feeling, help you connect with your baby, and support you and your support person through the stay.

How I can help

  • Before birth: if your baby may need the NICU, we'll find out what to expect at your hospital and plan for your baby's first hours.
  • The first days: getting ready for your first visit, caring for your own recovery, and starting to pump if that's your plan.
  • During the stay: questions for rounds, ways to bond before and after you can hold your baby, pumping support, and help for partners and siblings.
  • Going home: getting ready for discharge, and support in the first weeks at home.

Lactation support in the NICU

Pumping for a baby in the NICU often looks different from how we picture feeding our babies. From hand expressing colostrum in the first hours to a pumping plan that leaves room for sleep and recovery, I'll help you work toward your feeding goals.

Where I work

I support families with babies in any Milwaukee-area NICU, including Children's Wisconsin, Aurora Sinai, Aurora West Allis, Ascension Columbia St. Mary's, and Ascension St. Joseph. Visits can happen at the hospital (as policy allows), at home, or virtually.

Other NICU support in Milwaukee

  • March of Dimes NICU Family Support at Children's Wisconsin: education sessions and events for NICU parents, siblings, and grandparents. Ask your baby's nurse to connect you.
  • Hand to Hold: online support groups and peer mentors who have been through the NICU, in English and Spanish.
  • Ronald McDonald House Charities Eastern Wisconsin: a place to stay near Children's Wisconsin during a long NICU stay. Ask your baby's social worker about a referral.

More resources for NICU families, including mental health and feeding support, are in the Community Resource Guide.
